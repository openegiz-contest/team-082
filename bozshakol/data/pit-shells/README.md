# Pit shells (Lerchs–Grossmann)

`pit_shells.json` is produced by `python3 bozshakol/tools/pit_optimize.py` (needs numpy and scipy; the twin itself only reads the file).

- **Method.** The Lerchs–Grossmann ultimate-pit problem is a maximum-closure problem. It is solved exactly as a minimum cut (Picard, 1976), the formulation behind the pseudoflow solvers of current pit optimisers. Nested shells come from the same problem with the copper price multiplied by revenue factors 0.35 … 1.0.
- **Block model.** 30 × 30 × 30 m blocks around each pit. Cu and Au come from the GeoMet drill-hole field (Zenodo 7051975, CC BY 4.0, another porphyry deposit), re-levelled to the Bozshakol reserve grade 0.38 % Cu (KAZ Minerals Annual Report 2015). Grades fade outside an assumed orebody cone. Its single shape for both pits is tuned only so that the two ultimate pits together hold about the measured and indicated resources (936 Mt) at a strip ratio near the life-of-mine 0.7 t/t. **This is a synthetic block model, not the Bozshakol pit design.**
- **Economics (assumptions).** Net Cu 4.3 M KZT/t and net Au 1.4 M KZT/oz (as in `sim/params.py`); recovery 79 % Cu and 54 % Au (2023 actuals). Mining costs 900 KZT/t. Processing and G&A cost 4 400 KZT/t, which puts the break-even grade at the twin's low-grade cut-off of 0.13 % Cu. The overall slope is 42°. The domain is 270 m deep for the Central pit (the 2040 design depth in the 2023 mine plan) and 150 m for the East pit.
- **Use in the twin.** For each pit the strip ratio of its ultimate pit is the design strip ratio for exposed-reserve accounting (`sim/params.py`, `PITS[*].strip_t_t`). The full result is the attribute `pit_shells` of the twins `pit-central` and `pit-east`. The 3D control room draws it under the layer «контур LG».
